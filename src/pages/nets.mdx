A net is a scheduled on-air meeting on a specific frequency. Kind of like a group call over amateur radio. Some nets are more informal, with just a bunch of people talking at once, and some are more formal, where individuals have to request permission to speak. The ham directing the net is called Net Control.

## Attending a net

Getting on the air can be scary for new hams, known as "mic fright". This is completely normal at the beginning, but you shouldn't be afraid to talk to others over the air. On a few ragchew nets, you can regularly hear some other experienced hams, who have been doing this for decades, double over each other (transmit at the same time, ensuring nobody can be heard), or go on and time out the repeater multiple times in a row (when one talks so long that the repeater refuses to continue to transmitting for a short period). Don't worry about making a mistake, everybody starts somewhere and we're just glad that you are trying to get into the hobby. To help make getting on the air as easy as possible, here is a short script of what to expect in most nets. 

Formal / directed nets:

1. At the scheduled time, net control does introductions and says preamble
2. Net control asks for check ins (people who want to attend)
3. When net control asks for your category _(e.g. "members of MSJ ARC, please check in now", or "can I get callsigns with endings Alpha through Lima?", or "Any late check-ins, please check in now")_, respond with your callsign (or, in some nets, your callsign ending, the part after the number in your callsign). It's sometimes ideal to use the phonetic alphabet when saying your callsign.
4. When net control calls you ("callsign ending with Uniform Zulu Uniform?"), introduce yourself with the standard details for your particular net (you can listen to how others respond)
5. Say whether you have traffic (things to bring up in the net)
6. Continue following net control's instructions and net's structure
7. Before saying something, say your callsign first, and wait for net control to call you to go ahead.
8. If net control says you can "go direct", it means you can directly talk with someone else on the net without going through net control each time. 
9. At the end of your message, it is customary to identify yourself and return the net to Net Control. ("KN6UZU, back to you, net control.")

For getting more used to talking on the air, you can ask local nets whether you can get practice being net control for a day. The Tuesday Night Youth Net offers opportunities for other youth to practice being net control.

## Echolink

[Learn more at www.echolink.org](https://www.echolink.org/)

Echolink is an online app that allows you to access certain repeaters from your phone, over the internet. Your can listen in and transmit on repeaters across the world, all without being in range of that repeater or needing a radio. You do need a license to use this tool.
