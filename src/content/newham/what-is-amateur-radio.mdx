Amateur radio (often called **ham radio**) is a licensed hobby and public service. With a radio and an FCC amateur license, you can talk across town, across the country, or around the world — without the internet or a cell network.

You do **not** need a license to listen. You **do** need a license to transmit.

## What you can do

- Chat locally on VHF/UHF (often through [repeaters](/repeaters))
- Talk longer distances on HF (“shortwave”) once you upgrade your license
- Use digital modes, Morse code (optional!), satellites, and even the International Space Station
- Build antennas, kits, and station accessories
- Practice the skills used in emergency and public-service communications

Our club call sign is **W6MSJ**. Members operate club equipment and their own radios under FCC rules.

## How it is different from other radio

| Service | License to transmit? | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Amateur (ham) | Yes (FCC amateur license) | Hobby, experimentation, emergency comms |
| FRS / walkie-talkies | No | Short-range consumer radios |
| CB | No | 11-meter voice, limited power |
| Cell phones | No | Commercial networks that can go down |

Ham radio is not a replacement for 911, and it is not an unlicensed “free-for-all.” Operators identify with a call sign, follow band plans, and stay off frequencies they are not allowed to use.

## Who uses it?

Students, teachers, engineers, first responders, and retirees. Ages range from elementary school through 90+. Many MSJ ARC members earned their first license as students, with no prior electronics background.
