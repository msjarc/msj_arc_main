Congratulations — you have a call sign. You are allowed to transmit on the frequencies your license class permits. A few habits will make the first weeks much easier.

## Look up your license

Confirm your call sign and class in the [FCC ULS](https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/universal-licensing-system) and on [QRZ](https://www.qrz.com). Set up a QRZ profile when you are ready; other operators will look you up.

## A first radio

Many Technicians start with a **handheld (HT)** on 2 meters and 70 centimeters. Before you buy:

- Ask the club what hits local [repeaters](/repeaters)
- Avoid ultra-cheap radios with no support if you can
- A programming cable and a second-hand mobile/base later will matter more than RGB lighting

You can listen before you transmit. Listening is how you learn the local custom.

## Get on a repeater

Program the output frequency, offset, and tone. Identify with your call sign. A simple first contact is:

> “This is KN6ABC, listening.”

(Use **your** call sign.) If nobody answers, that is normal. Try a [net](/nets) — nets are designed for check-ins.

Identify at least every 10 minutes during a conversation and at the end. Be a good guest on a repeater: leave pauses, do not rag-chew over a busy machine, and yield to emergency traffic.

## Join the club on the air

Come to meetings, check into the club net when we have one, and ask for help programming your radio. Members would rather help you get it right than have you guess.

## Keep going

Upgrade to General when you want HF. Try a new mode. Help the next student who is still on [HamStudy](https://hamstudy.org). That is the whole hobby, looping.
