In the United States, amateur licenses are issued by the FCC. There is **no minimum age**. The exam is multiple choice. You do not have to build a radio or pass a Morse code test.

## License classes

| Class | Exam | What it unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Technician** | 35 questions | Most VHF/UHF privileges, plus limited HF. The usual first license. |
| **General** | Extra 35 questions | Much more HF — the bands used for long-distance contacts. |
| **Amateur Extra** | Extra 50 questions | All amateur privileges. |

Most new operators start with Technician, get on the air, then upgrade when they want HF.

## How to study

The question pools are public. You are studying the actual exam questions, not a mystery test.

- [HamStudy.org](https://hamstudy.org) — practice exams and spaced repetition
- [ARRL Getting Licensed](https://www.arrl.org/getting-licensed) — overview of the process
- Club study sessions — ask at a meeting; we help members prepare

You do not need to memorize every answer on day one. Practice until you are consistently above the passing score (74%).

## Taking the exam

Exams are given by Volunteer Examiners (VEs), often at clubs, hamfests, or online sessions.

1. Study until practice tests are comfortably passing.
2. Find a session (we can point you to local VEs).
3. Bring photo ID. Expect a small session fee (often around $15).
4. If you pass, the FCC application fee for a new license is currently **$35**.
5. Your call sign shows up in the [FCC ULS](https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/universal-licensing-system) when it is granted.

## After you pass

You are not on the air until the FCC issues the license and call sign. That can take a few days. When it appears in ULS, continue to [once you are licensed](/newham/once-licensed).
